Field Observation and Botanical Interpretation

On October 23, 2026, a shallow irrigation ditch was constructed near the mango tree adjacent to the driveway and compost piles at 3741 Poka Place. A hose and nozzle were positioned to create a continuously circulating pool of water that maintained a counterclockwise rotational flow for approximately 15-minute intervals.

Fresh purslane and other recently harvested plant materials were placed within the flow path of the water. As the water moved continuously through and around these materials, it remained in contact with plant tissues containing minerals, sugars, amino acids, water, and other organic compounds produced through photosynthesis.

The rotating flow helped maintain mixing and circulation within the pooled water, increasing contact between the water, fresh plant material, soil surface, compost piles, and root zone of the mango tree. This created a localized system in which moisture and organic matter were continuously redistributed throughout the treatment area.

From a BOT 101 perspective, the plant material incorporated into the water was originally produced through photosynthesis and the Calvin Cycle. Carbon dioxide absorbed from the atmosphere was converted into sugars and other organic compounds that became part of the plant tissues. By introducing this fresh biomass into the irrigation system, water acted as a transport medium connecting photosynthetically produced plant matter with soil, compost, microorganisms, and plant roots.

The addition of dissolved sugars, minerals, and other solutes may influence water potential within the immediate environment. As discussed in class, increased solute concentrations decrease water potential, affecting the movement of water by osmosis. Water movement into plant cells contributes to turgor pressure, which supports plant structure, growth, and physiological function.

The irrigation event also rehydrated nearby compost piles, providing moisture necessary for microbial metabolism and decomposition. Through these processes, organic matter can be broken down and nutrients recycled back into forms that may become available to plants.
